Record everything your PC plays (no install)

On Windows, Chrome and Edge can capture the full system mix: music apps, games, Zoom, YouTube and alert sounds. You keep hearing it in your headphones or speakers as normal.

  1. Open this file in Chrome or Microsoft Edge. Press Ctrl+O in the browser, or drag the file onto a browser window.
  2. Click Share PC sound… and the browser's share picker opens.
  3. Choose the Entire screen tab and click your screen.
  4. Switch on Share system audio at the bottom left of the picker, then click Share.
  5. Press the red button. Everything you hear is recorded.

Only the sound is kept; the screen picture is discarded straight away. A “sharing your screen” bar appears at the bottom of Windows while connected; click Stop sharing there or on this page when you finish. To record just one browser tab, pick the Chrome Tab (or Edge Tab) pane instead and leave Share tab audio on. Firefox can't capture system sound, so use Chrome or Edge.

Record through an input device

Use this when the share method isn't available, or to record in Firefox. Windows needs a recording device that carries what the speakers play. Use Stereo Mix if your PC has it; otherwise install the free VB-CABLE.

Option A · Stereo Mix (built in on many PCs)

  1. Press Win+R, type mmsys.cpl, press Enter. Open the Recording tab.
  2. Right-click an empty area and tick Show Disabled Devices.
  3. Right-click Stereo Mix › Enable. If it isn't listed, your sound card doesn't have it; use Option B.
  4. Here, click Connect, allow microphone access, and pick Stereo Mix.

Option B · VB-CABLE (free virtual cable)

  1. Download the driver pack from vb-audio.com/Cable and extract the zip.
  2. Right-click VBCABLE_Setup_x64.exe › Run as administrator › Install Driver, then restart the PC.
  3. In Settings › System › Sound › Output, choose CABLE Input.
  4. To keep hearing it: open mmsys.cpl › Recording, double-click CABLE Output › Listen tab, tick Listen to this device, choose your headphones or speakers under Playback through this device, then OK.
  5. Here, click Connect and pick CABLE Output.

Leave “Monitor” off here; Windows already plays the sound to you, and Monitor would double it. If Connect says access was blocked, open Settings › Privacy & security › Microphone and turn on Let desktop apps access your microphone.
